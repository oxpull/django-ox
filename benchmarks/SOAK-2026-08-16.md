# Soak and chaos results, 2026-08-16

django-ox 0.1.0 under sustained mixed load and repeated worker SIGKILLs on
PostgreSQL 16. The harness is `soak.py` in this directory (methodology in
its docstring and in [README.md](README.md)); the raw data, including
every sample, every kill event, and every assertion, is in
[soak-results-raw-2026-08-16.json](soak-results-raw-2026-08-16.json).
That file is the source of truth and this document is written from it.
All scenarios and all assertions are reported. None were discarded.

Exact invocation: `soak.py --seed 51` (run started 2026-08-15T23:01:44Z,
finished 2026-08-15T23:23:25Z). A prior `--smoke` validation run is in
`soak-results-raw-2026-08-16-SMOKE.json`; smoke numbers are never quoted.

## What this measures

Every task execution writes phase rows (started / completed / failed)
with a per-execution nonce to a `soak_ledger` side table on the worker
thread's own autocommit connection. A started row with no exit row is an
execution that died mid-body; more than one completed row for a task is a
measured double execution. At-least-once vs exactly-once behavior is
therefore read off side effects after the fact, not assumed from task
rows. All integrity assertions run against the database and this ledger.

## Environment and parameters

- Apple M1 Max, 10 logical cores, 64 GiB RAM; macOS 26.5.2
- Python 3.12.13, Django 6.0.8, psycopg 3.3.4, django-ox 0.1.0 (editable)
- PostgreSQL 16.14 (postgres:16 image) in Docker Desktop, container
  `plow-pg`, localhost:54329, database `soak_ox`
- Workers: `ox_worker --concurrency 4 --interval 0.2`, 3 processes
  (crash-window scenario: 1 process, concurrency 2)
- Backend OPTIONS (non-default, chosen so kill-reclaim cycles fit inside
  a scenario): `LOCK_TIMEOUT` 15 s, `BACKOFF_INITIAL` 2 s, `BACKOFF_MAX`
  10 s, `MAX_ATTEMPTS` 3. The worker derives a reap interval of 7.5 s
  from that lock timeout. The asserted reclaim bound is
  15 + 7.5 + 15 margin = 37.5 s. With the default 300 s lock timeout the
  same behavior scales to 300 + up to 30 + slack.
- Producer mix per enqueue: quick 70% (sleep 10-50 ms), medium 15%
  (200 ms), slow 5% (1.5 s), flaky_once 5% (fails attempt 1, succeeds on
  retry), doomed 5% (fails every attempt; must end FAILED).

## Steady (no chaos): baseline correctness and resource behavior

21,600 tasks enqueued by three producer processes over 12 minutes;
producer-reported counts match the table exactly. The ledger recorded
24,831 executions, which reconciles to the row count exactly:
21,600 first attempts + 1,125 flaky_once retries + 2,106 doomed retries
(2 extra attempts x 1,053). 3,231 retry warnings in the worker logs
match (1,125 + 2,106).

- **Exactly-once observed**: completions histogram {1: 20,547}. Zero
  double completions, zero interrupted executions, zero phantom
  successes (SUCCESSFUL rows with no ledger completion).
- **No spurious reclaims**: zero reaper activity across 12 minutes, i.e.
  the 15 s lock timeout never misfired against live tasks up to 1.5 s
  long, and no lock was ever older than 27.5 s in any of 138 samples.
- **Queue depth**: max READY backlog 24 rows; the fleet (12 slots)
  absorbed the ~34 executions/s continuously.
- **Latency** (enqueue to finish, first-attempt successes only; retries
  excluded because they measure the deliberate backoff schedule):

| Type | n | p50 | p95 | p99 | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| quick | 15,130 | 0.181 s | 0.329 s | 0.412 s | 0.555 s |
| medium | 3,228 | 0.352 s | 0.506 s | 0.588 s | 0.701 s |
| slow | 1,064 | 1.647 s | 1.800 s | 1.890 s | 1.983 s |
| all | 19,422 | 0.211 s | 1.541 s | 1.722 s | 1.983 s |

  Sleep durations are included (slow sleeps 1.5 s), so the queueing
  overhead is the difference: roughly 150-350 ms at this load, dominated
  by the 0.2 s poll interval when all slots are briefly busy.
- **RSS**: each worker settles near 58.4 MB after Django boot and ends
  at 58.7-58.9 MB after 12 minutes and ~8,300 executions per process.
  Quartile means after the boot transient (worker 0: 58.4 / 58.7 / 58.7 /
  58.7 MB) show growth confined to the first quarter. No leak signal at
  this horizon; longer horizons were not measured.

## Chaos: 15 SIGKILLs under the same load

Same load for 9 minutes while the orchestrator SIGKILLed a random worker
every 20-45 s (5 kills per slot, first 23:14:17Z, last 23:22:21Z) and
restarted it 2-5 s later. 16,198 tasks; producer counts match the table
exactly (two producers reported 5,399 rather than 5,400: their pacing
loop hit the deadline one enqueue early; nothing was lost).

- **37 claims were interrupted by kills**: 33 executions died mid-body
  (ledger: started row, no exit row) and 4 died in the window between
  the claim UPDATE and the task body's first statement (row shows a
  consumed attempt with no ledger started row). All 37 were reclaimed
  and re-executed; none were abandoned; every one of the 16,198 tasks
  reached a terminal state.
- **Reclaim latency**: for the 33 measurable interruptions, time from
  interrupted body start to the re-execution's body start (both DB-clock
  ledger timestamps, so container/host clock drift cannot pollute them)
  ranged 15.49-20.07 s. All well inside the 37.5 s asserted bound, and
  in fact inside LOCK_TIMEOUT + one reap pass (22.5 s).
- **Bookkeeping under SIGKILL**: attempts never exceeded max_attempts
  (0 violations in 16,198 rows) and `attempts == len(worker_ids)` held
  on every row, including the 4 claim-window kills. The increment-4
  design decision to fold all per-attempt bookkeeping into the single
  claim UPDATE is what makes that invariant kill-proof, and this is the
  first time it was exercised by real SIGKILLs at load.
- **Double completions: zero.** See the honest caveat below; this is an
  observation about this run, not a guarantee.
- **Failure accounting**: all 779 doomed tasks FAILED with attempts 3;
  all 841 flaky_once tasks SUCCEEDED (no task lost enough attempts to
  kills to change its outcome); zero organic failures elsewhere.
- **Latency under chaos** barely moved against steady: p50 0.217 s vs
  0.211 s, p95 1.538 s vs 1.541 s, max 2.343 s vs 1.983 s; max backlog
  27 vs 24. Fifteen process kills cost the fleet no visible throughput
  at this utilization (~36% of slots).

## Crash-window: forced kill while holding claimed tasks

One worker (concurrency 2), four `slow_hold` tasks that sleep 8 s. The
worker claimed two and was SIGKILLed ~1 s into their sleeps (2 RUNNING
rows confirmed in the database at kill time), then restarted 3 s later.

- The restarted worker picked up the two untouched READY tasks
  immediately and finished them.
- The two stale RUNNING rows were reclaimed and re-executed 20.05 s
  after their interrupted body start (lock expiry at 15 s plus the next
  reap pass), and both finished SUCCESSFUL.
- Final ledger: exactly one completion per task ({1: 4}), 2 interrupted
  started rows from the killed pid, attempts == len(worker_ids)
  throughout, expected-interrupts assertion (2) met exactly.

## Findings

The documented guarantees held. Across 16 SIGKILLs, 39 interrupted
claims (37 in chaos, 2 in crash-window), and 37,802 total tasks, every task reached a terminal state,
every interrupted claim was re-executed or (never needed) abandoned
within the reclaim bound, attempts stayed bounded, and the
attempts/worker_ids invariant survived every kill. No bug was found and
no code change resulted from this run. The harness is built to fail
loudly (any failed assertion exits non-zero and is recorded in the JSON),
so "no bug" is a checked outcome, not an absence of looking.

Negative findings and caveats, so nobody over-reads this:

- **Zero double completions does not mean exactly-once.** The design is
  at-least-once; a kill landing between a task's final side effect and
  its status UPDATE would produce a legitimate duplicate execution. That
  window is milliseconds wide and 16 kills never hit it. The 39
  re-executions are the at-least-once machinery working as documented;
  task idempotence guidance in docs/production.md stands.
- **The 4 claim-window kills are expected behavior**, not losses: the
  attempt was consumed atomically at claim, the reaper returned the row,
  and it re-executed. They are reported separately because they are
  invisible to the ledger's started rows and only the claim bookkeeping
  accounts for them.
- **Non-default tuning.** LOCK_TIMEOUT 15 s, backoff 2/10 s, poll 0.2 s
  were chosen to compress kill-reclaim cycles into a 9 minute scenario.
  Reclaim latency scales with LOCK_TIMEOUT; at the 300 s default a dead
  worker's tasks wait up to ~330 s, as documented.
- **Single machine, localhost database, ~36% fleet utilization.** The
  bench README's environment caveats apply unchanged. Saturation
  behavior (utilization near 100%, deep backlogs) was not measured here.
- **RSS horizon is 12 minutes.** Flat after boot, but a multi-hour or
  multi-day leak would not show at this length.
- **Latency includes task sleep time** and is measured enqueued_at to
  finished_at on first-attempt successes only. Both timestamps come from
  the same host's clock (producers and workers share the machine).
