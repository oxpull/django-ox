# Soak and chaos results, 2026-09-11

django-ox 1.1.0 under sustained mixed load and repeated worker SIGKILLs on
PostgreSQL 16. The harness is `soak.py` in this directory (methodology in
its docstring and in [README.md](README.md)); the raw data, including
every sample, every kill event, and every assertion, is in
[soak-results-raw-2026-09-11.json](soak-results-raw-2026-09-11.json).
That file is the source of truth and this document is written from it.
All scenarios and all assertions are reported. None were discarded.

Exact invocation: `soak.py --seed 350589` (run started 2026-09-11T17:01:24Z, finished
2026-09-11T17:23:05Z). The seed is the one the [2026-09-01 run](SOAK-2026-09-01.md)
used, so the kill schedule is the same.

## Environment and parameters

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Host | Apple M1 Max, 10 logical CPUs, 64 GB |
| OS | macOS 26.6.2 |
| Python | 3.12.13 |
| Django | 6.0.8 |
| django-ox | 1.1.0 |
| psycopg | 3.3.5 |
| PostgreSQL | 16.14 (Debian), in Docker, port 54329 |
| Reclaim bound | 37.5 s (`LOCK_TIMEOUT` 15 s plus one reap interval, with a margin) |

Three producer processes and three worker processes per load scenario. Each
producer targets 10 tasks per second, 30 combined, across a mixed workload of
quick, medium, slow and deliberately failing task types. The worker ran with
`LOCK_TIMEOUT` 15 s, `BACKOFF_INITIAL` 2 s and `BACKOFF_MAX` 10 s so that
reclaims and retries happen inside the run; the shipped defaults are 300 s,
5 s and 600 s.

## Scenarios

| Scenario | Load | Kills | Tasks | Terminal states |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| steady | 720.6 s | 0 | 21,600 | 20,426 SUCCESSFUL, 1,174 FAILED |
| chaos | 540.6 s | 17 | 16,200 | 15,316 SUCCESSFUL, 884 FAILED |
| crash-window | 28.1 s | 1 | 4 | 4 SUCCESSFUL |

Every FAILED row in steady and chaos is a `doomed` task, a type written to
fail on every attempt so that the retry ceiling is exercised. In both
scenarios the count of doomed tasks equals the count of FAILED rows
exactly, and there were no unexplained failures.

Total: 37,804 tasks, 18 kills, 21.5 minutes under load.

## Double executions

None this run. Execution is at-least-once: a worker killed between finishing
a task and recording the outcome leaves no evidence that the work happened,
and the only safe reading of that state is to run the task again. Whether a
kill lands inside that window is a matter of timing; the
[2026-09-01 run](SOAK-2026-09-01.md), on the same kill schedule, recorded one
such execution and attributed it to its kill. The property that holds across
both runs, and that the harness asserts, is that a second execution is only
ever attributable to a kill: no task ran twice without one.

## Latency

Latency of successful first attempts (the quick, medium and slow types),
seconds:

| Scenario | p50 | p95 | p99 | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| steady | 0.126 | 1.527 | 1.637 | 1.743 |
| chaos | 0.124 | 1.525 | 1.641 | 1.738 |

Killing a worker every 20 to 45 seconds moved p50 by two milliseconds and
p99 by four. The queue depth stayed bounded throughout: the largest READY
backlog observed was 24 rows in steady and 25 under chaos. Over the
twelve-minute steady scenario, where a worker lives long enough to measure it,
memory settled near 59 MB after the first minute and moved under 1 MB after
that.

## Findings

1. Every one of 37,804 tasks reached a terminal state. Nothing was lost.
2. All 27 executions interrupted by a kill were re-executed, none were
   abandoned, and none were left unresolved. The slowest reclaim was
   19.6 seconds against a documented bound of 37.5.
3. No task executed twice in this run. The harness asserts the stronger property, that
   a second execution is only ever attributable to a kill, and it held.
4. Retry counts stayed bounded on every row, the worker-id count matched the
   attempt count on every row, and no row was marked LOST, in every scenario.
5. Repeated kills did not degrade latency or grow the backlog.

## Caveats

This is one host, one database, and one 21.5-minute run at roughly 30 tasks per second. It says nothing about a cluster, about other databases,
or about load an order of magnitude higher. Throughput is measured on the
[benchmarks page](https://oxpull.com/django-ox/benchmarks/). What this run
measures is whether the documented durability behaviour holds while workers
are being killed underneath it.
